Principles over products

This list reflects what we know works today, but tools change constantly. What matters is understanding the principles behind these choices:

  • Open-source when possible: You can audit code, verify claims, and contribute improvements.
  • Self-hosted options: You should be able to control your own infrastructure.
  • End-to-end encryption: Only people with encryption keys can read your data.
  • Zero-knowledge design: Service providers should have no access to your data even if subpoenaed.
  • Active maintenance: Tools should have recent commits, active issue response, security updates.
  • Transparent practices: Organizations should be clear about their business model, funding, and data practices.

We evaluate tools against these principles regularly and update recommendations as the landscape changes.

Evaluate tools yourself

We teach frameworks for critically evaluating privacy tools in our workshops. Ask: Who benefits from collecting your data? Who has access if they're hacked? Can you audit the code? Is the organization transparent about funding? Are there recent security audits?

Good security requires a healthy skepticism of vendors, including us. Our job is to teach you to think critically, not to tell you which tool to use.
