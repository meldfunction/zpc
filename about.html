About us

We're a nonprofit dedicated to helping people and organizations understand and practice privacy as an ongoing discipline.

Our mission

Zen Privacy Collective exists to preserve human dignity through informed, intentional privacy practice. We believe privacy is not paranoia, compliance theater, or individual responsibility alone. Privacy is a collective discipline: knowing what to protect, why it matters, and building systems that let people work safely together.

We teach privacy as literacy and practice. We help organizations design governance and infrastructure that protect what matters. We contribute to open-source tools that make privacy-preserving work possible.

Our values

Intention over fear

We're grounded and practical. Privacy practice starts with knowing your actual threats and building proportionate responses.

Literacy and infrastructure

Good tools without understanding don't last. We build both: education that sticks and infrastructure that lets you act on it.

Collective care

Privacy isn't individual responsibility. We help teams and organizations care for shared security and build trust together.

Open practice

We contribute research, tools, and methodologies openly. Privacy works better when practices are shared and tested.

Dignity first

Privacy protects human dignity. We work with marginalized communities and those doing sensitive work.

Accessibility

Privacy can't be just for the technically skilled or wealthy. We keep our workshops affordable and our tools simple.

Who we are

Zen Privacy Collective brings together educators, security practitioners, systems thinkers, and organizers. We come from backgrounds in digital rights advocacy (EFF), secure infrastructure (Proton), open-source intelligence (OSINT community), and hands-on security work (DEFCON). We're grounded in real experience protecting sensitive work and building trustworthy organizations.

How we're governed

Zen Privacy Collective operates using sociocracy principles, also known as dynamic governance. This means:

  • Consent-based decisions: We make decisions based on consent, not consensus or top-down authority. Objections are treated as gifts that improve our work.
  • Circular governance: We organize around circles of decision-making (strategy, programs, operations) rather than traditional hierarchies.
  • Double-linking: Each circle has representatives in the circles above and below, ensuring alignment and communication flow.
  • Equivalence: Every member has voice in decisions that affect their role. Authority is distributed, not concentrated.

This structure keeps us accountable, responsive, and aligned with our values. It also means everyone contributes to shaping what we do.
